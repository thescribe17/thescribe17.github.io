### Passage by Connie Willis

**Format:** Book

**My rating:** 3 of 5 stars

### Blurb

A tunnel, a light, a door.

And beyond it... the unimaginable.

Dr Joanna Lander is a psychologist specialising in near-death experiences. When a neurologist develops a method of artificially creating those experiences, she has an opportunity to explore questions she has long pursued.

But the deeper she investigates, the more disturbing the answers become.

### My review

This was the strangest book.

It started out repetitive but held my attention.

Then the pace increased dramatically and I hated having to put it down.

Then, at a major turning point in the story, it slipped back into repetition again.

Despite that, it was a genuinely good book.

I read all seven hundred-plus pages, which says something in itself.

The author carried the reader to an extraordinary point in the story, but in my opinion kept going for too long afterwards.

Instead of stopping and allowing the impact of the story to settle, she continued beyond the point where I felt the novel should have ended.

The ending had a clear purpose and I understood what she was trying to achieve.

I simply felt the story had already reached its natural conclusion.

Still, it provided a valuable reminder about storytelling.

When a story is over, it's over.
