### One Day in the Life of Ivan Denisovich by Aleksandr Solzhenitsyn

**Format:** Book

**My rating:** 4 of 5 stars

### My review

*One Day in the Life of Ivan Denisovich* tells the story of a man sentenced to ten years in a Soviet labour camp for a crime he did not commit.

The story is not filled with suspense and shocking twists.

Instead, it is driven by character.

The reader experiences a single day in Ivan's life and comes to appreciate the hardships, routines and small victories that make survival possible.

The one thing that became very clear to me was how humans adapt to their circumstances.

When a person can find happiness in a few grams of stale bread and an extra ladle of watery soup, it puts modern life into perspective.

The book is written as one long chapter with no scene breaks.

At first I found this irritating, but eventually I adjusted.

I also found parts of the writing confusing, especially the naming conventions and shifts in perspective.

Even so, I found the story fascinating.

It made me wonder how well I would cope in a similar situation.

I suspect not terribly well.

This is a book I would never have picked up without a recommendation.

Once again, it proves that a book cannot be judged by its cover.

Recommended.
