When readers pick up a Cat & Mouse Adventure, one of the first things they encounter is Cherryville. Although the town doesn't exist on any real map, it has become one of my favourite places to visit while writing.

Cherryville started as a simple backdrop for an adventure story, but over time it became much more than that. The school, the river, the general store, the library, the cemetery, and even the houses all grew organically as the series developed.

One thing I love about small-town stories is that everybody knows everybody else. That creates opportunities for interesting friendships, misunderstandings, mysteries and adventures. A local librarian might have the clue the children need. A teacher might encourage them to ask the right question. A shopkeeper might know a story nobody else remembers.

The town has also allowed me to create recurring characters who feel like part of a larger community rather than people who only appear for one book.

When I'm planning a story, I often spend time thinking about Cherryville itself. What does it look like? What does it sound like? What is happening when Cat and Mouse aren't around?

For me, a good setting should feel alive, and Cherryville continues to grow with every new adventure.
