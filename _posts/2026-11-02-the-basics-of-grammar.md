Grammar doesn't have to be complicated. As writers, we use these building blocks every day, often without thinking about them. However, when editing our work, it can be helpful to understand exactly what role a word is playing in a sentence.

This simple reference guide covers some of the most common parts of speech.

## Adjective

An **adjective** is a describing word. It adds meaning to a noun or pronoun.

Examples:

- **fast** car
- **windy** day
- **happy** child

## Adverb

An **adverb** adds meaning to a verb, an adjective, or another adverb.

Examples:

- **very** humid
- **too** hot
- She ran **quickly**.

## Noun

A **noun** is the name of a person, place, thing, or idea.

Examples:

- **Dylan**
- **Australia**
- **computer**
- **happiness**

Example sentences:

- Dylan came to Australia in 2018.
- Messing around on his computer brings him happiness.

## Pronoun

A **pronoun** is a word used in place of a noun.

Examples:

- **he**
- **she**
- **they**
- **mine**

Example sentence:

- **He** is **mine**.

## Verb

A **verb** is a doing, being, or having word.

Examples:

- The dog **ate** his food.
- We **are** happy.
- They **have** a new teacher.

Understanding these basic parts of speech can make editing easier and help you spot problems in your writing more quickly. Even experienced writers occasionally need a refresher, which is why I've created these grammar notes as a handy reference for myself and anyone else who finds them useful.
