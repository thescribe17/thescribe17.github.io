People often imagine that mysteries begin with clues. For me, they usually begin with a question.

What would happen if a group of children discovered something unusual?

What if they misunderstood what they had found?

And what if that misunderstanding led them into an adventure?

Those kinds of questions often become the starting point for a Cat & Mouse story.

Once I have a question, I begin building the mystery backwards. I work out what really happened before deciding what the characters believe happened. The difference between those two things creates suspense.

I also enjoy placing clues throughout the story. Some clues are obvious. Others are hidden in conversations, descriptions or seemingly unimportant details.

One of the most important rules I follow is that the solution should feel fair. Readers may not solve the mystery before the characters do, but when the answer is revealed, they should be able to look back and realise the clues were there all along.

Of course, Cat and Mouse never investigate alone.

Mouse tends to notice details other people overlook. Cat pushes forward whenever she discovers a mystery. Del provides enthusiasm, courage and the occasional unexpected idea. Leila loves research and facts, often uncovering clues hidden in books, records and local history.

Together they bring very different strengths to every adventure, and each mystery becomes more interesting because no two characters approach problems in quite the same way.

The result is part mystery, part adventure, and hopefully a lot of fun.

And it all starts with a simple question:

**What if...?**
