This is one of those grammar problems that manages to trip me up every time. Just when I think I've mastered it, I find myself staring at a sentence wondering whether I should use *laid*, *lain*, *lay* or *lie*.

To save myself the trouble of looking it up repeatedly, I've put together this quick reference guide. Hopefully it will help other writers too.

## Lay = Put Something Down

| Tense | Word |
|---------|---------|
| Present | lay |
| Past | laid |
| Past Participle | laid |

Examples:

- I **lay** the book on the table.
- Yesterday I **laid** the book on the table.
- I have **laid** the book on the table.

## Lie = Rest or Recline

| Tense | Word |
|---------|---------|
| Present | lie |
| Past | lay |
| Past Participle | lain |

Examples:

- I **lie** down every afternoon.
- Yesterday I **lay** down.
- I have **lain** awake all night.

## Quick Memory Trick

**Laid** = something was placed somewhere.

> She **laid** the keys on the desk.

**Lain** = someone rested.

> The cat has **lain** in the sun all day.

## The Most Common Mistake

❌ Yesterday I **laid** down for a nap.

✅ Yesterday I **lay** down for a nap.

Nothing was being placed anywhere. The action was resting.
